## Why this exists at all

**This document was written for a licence the project no longer carries, and the reason it
exists has largely gone with it.** Between 2026-07-29 and 2026-08-15 the project was published
under PolyForm Noncommercial 1.0.0, with commercial use licensed separately by written
agreement (ADR-0055). The Maintainer could only grant such a licence over code he held
sufficient rights in, and under plain inbound-equals-outbound a contribution would have
arrived on *noncommercial* terms and could not lawfully have been included in a commercial
grant — so every commercial licence issued afterwards would have been partly unauthorised.
That was the entire reason this was a CLA and not a DCO.

The project is now published under the **Apache License 2.0** ([`LICENSE`](./LICENSE),
ADR-0063, which supersedes ADR-0055). Apache-2.0 §5 places every contribution under the same
terms as the Work, there is no separate commercial grant to authorise, and **a DCO would now
be sufficient**. Whether to replace this agreement with one is an open decision for the
Maintainer, recorded in ADR-0063 and listed under
[Notes for the reviewer](#notes-for-the-reviewer).

Until that decision is taken, `CONTRIBUTING.md` requires this agreement and it is the
instrument that requirement points at. What it still buys, on top of a DCO, is a recorded and
explicit grant, and the ability to relicense the Work later without tracing every
contributor — a right the Maintainer has now needed twice in three weeks.

**What this is not.** It is not an assignment: you keep the copyright in your work. It does
not restrict what you do with your own contribution elsewhere — you may use, publish and
license it however you like. It makes no claim on anything you have not deliberately
submitted to this project.

## Who signs what

- **You are contributing on your own behalf** — sign this agreement.
- **You are contributing as part of your employment, on your employer's time or equipment, or
  using your employer's resources** — your employer may own the copyright. Section 4 requires
  you to have that cleared. In most such cases a **Corporate Contributor Licence Agreement
  (CCLA)**, signed by someone authorised to bind your employer, is what is actually needed.
  **No CCLA is drafted yet.** If this applies to you, say so before you open the pull request
  and the Maintainer will sort it out — a CCLA will go through the same legal review as this
  document, so allow time for it. Do not guess.

## How to sign

The procedure is in [`CONTRIBUTING.md`](./CONTRIBUTING.md) — in short: sign this document
electronically, or complete the block at the end and email it to `ddickmann81@gmail.com` with
the subject `CLA: latence-framework`. The Maintainer records the signature; a pull request is
not merged until that record exists. Administration is manual and deliberately so at this
size; automation is a later step.

## Provenance of this text

This document is a derivative of the **Apache Software Foundation Individual Contributor
License Agreement, V2.2** (`https://www.apache.org/licenses/icla.pdf`). The Apache text is
the industry-standard instrument and is reused here so that a reader who has signed one
before recognises what they are signing.

The substantive changes made in this derivative, all of which a reviewer should look at
first:

1. **The counterparty is a person, not a foundation.** "The Foundation" becomes "the
   Maintainer" throughout, along with the definition in Section 1 and the successors clause.
2. **The ASF's reciprocal covenant is removed.** The Apache preamble has the Foundation
   promise not to use Contributions "in a way that is contrary to the public benefit or
   inconsistent with its nonprofit status and bylaws". That promise cannot honestly be made
   here: the Maintainer is a person with a commercial interest, not a nonprofit bound by
   bylaws, and the promise was drafted for the latter. It is dropped, and Section 2 says
   plainly what does happen instead.
3. **Section 2 carries an explicit relicensing paragraph.** The Apache grant includes
   "sublicense", which arguably suffices, but the relicensing right was the entire reason
   this agreement was written, so it is stated rather than inferred.
4. **Section 2 adds a no-consideration / no-obligation sentence**, which the Apache text does
   not have.
5. **Apache-specific mechanics are dropped** — the "preferred Apache id", the public-profile
   starred fields, the ASF privacy-policy link, the `secretary@apache.org` address — and the
   signature block is reduced to what is actually needed here.
6. **A pointer to the AI-disclosure rule** was added to Section 5. It adds no obligation
   beyond the representation that was already there.
7. **Spelling is normalised to British English** ("Licence" as a noun), matching the rest of
   the repository. No operative wording changes with it.

Sections 3, 4, 6, 7 and 8, and the definitions in Section 1, are substantively the Apache
text with the counterparty swapped.

## Notes for the reviewer

The open points, stated rather than smoothed over:

1. **Reuse of the Apache text.** The ASF's contributor-agreements page publishes the ICLA but
   states no terms for its reuse by third parties, and the PDF carries no licence notice.
   Deriving from it is common practice across the industry; it is not, as far as the
   maintainer could verify, *explicitly* permitted in writing. Confirm the position, or
   redraft the affected sections.
2. **Governing law and venue.** The Apache ICLA contains no choice-of-law or forum clause and
   this derivative keeps that silence deliberately, rather than inventing an unreviewed one.
   Whether to add one — and which law and forum, given a German-resident maintainer and
   contributors who may be anywhere — is a decision for the reviewer, not for this draft.
3. **Enforceability of an emailed or electronically signed agreement** in the relevant
   jurisdiction, and whether a scanned signature, an e-signature service, or a typed name is
   sufficient. The procedure in `CONTRIBUTING.md` should be made to match whatever the answer
   is.
4. **Data protection.** Collecting and storing name, email, country and employer is personal
   data processing under the GDPR. The lawful basis, retention period, and whether a short
   privacy notice is required alongside this document all need confirming — Apache links a
   dedicated CLA privacy policy at this point in its own form, and this draft has only the
   paragraph under [Signature](#signature).
5. **The Corporate CLA.** Not drafted. Section 4 assumes one exists; until it does, an
   employer-owned contribution has no route to being merged. If corporate contributions are
   expected at all, the CCLA belongs in the same review round rather than a later one.
6. **Whether this agreement should exist at all.** ADR-0063 relicensed the project to
   Apache-2.0, whose §5 makes every contribution arrive under the Work's own licence. The gap
   this CLA was written to close no longer exists, and a DCO would suffice. The reviewer
   should advise on whether the residual benefits (a recorded explicit grant; the ability to
   relicense later without tracing contributors) justify asking a contributor to sign a
   two-page agreement — and, if not, on the DCO wording to replace it with. This supersedes
   the earlier open point about the retained Apache-2.0 subset in `latence-retrieval`, which
   is moot: the whole repository is Apache-2.0, so there is no longer a set of files licensed
   onward on different terms.
7. **Section 1's "and any other products owned or managed by the Maintainer"** follows the
   Apache wording, which is scoped to the Foundation's products. For a single maintainer with
   other projects, that phrase is broader than it looks. Narrowing it to this repository is a
   defensible alternative and the maintainer has no attachment to the wider form.
